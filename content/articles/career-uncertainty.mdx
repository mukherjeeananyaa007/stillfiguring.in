Career advice often assumes that clarity is a prerequisite for movement. First, decide what you want. Then, make a plan. Then, execute it with the calm focus of a person who has never opened a job board at midnight.

For many people, the order is reversed. You move first, notice what the movement reveals, and revise your understanding as you go. This is not a lack of ambition. It is a more honest account of how knowledge tends to arrive.

## The problem with finding your passion

The phrase sounds generous, but it can turn every ordinary job into evidence against your future. If your work does not feel like a calling, you wonder whether you have chosen the wrong life. If you enjoy several things, you are asked to rank them before you have had the chance to understand them.

A career is not one decision. It is a series of working hypotheses.

You can test a hypothesis by taking on a project, asking a better question in a meeting, or talking to somebody whose Tuesday resembles the Tuesday you think you want. Small experiments are less glamorous than reinvention, but they produce better evidence.

## Making room for a moving answer

There is a difference between drifting and remaining receptive. Drifting means letting circumstance make every decision for you. Receptiveness means listening for useful signals without demanding that they become a five-year plan by Friday.

Try asking a smaller question: what would make the next six months more interesting, more sustainable, or more educational? A smaller question does not shrink your life. It gives your attention somewhere it can actually go.

Certainty may come later. It may also keep changing shape. A good working life can hold that ambiguity. So can you.
