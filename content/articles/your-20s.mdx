There is a particular kind of pressure that arrives with your twenties: the sense that everyone else has received a map you somehow missed.

You see the evidence everywhere. Friends move cities with conviction. Someone from school announces an engagement. A former colleague has a five-year plan, a renovated kitchen, and the vocabulary to explain exactly how they got there. Meanwhile, you are still changing your mind about breakfast.

But a decade is not a waiting room. It is not a corridor between childhood and the serious business of becoming yourself. It is already your life, in all its unfinished and ordinary detail.

## The myth of the clean beginning

We are taught to imagine adulthood as a sequence of doors: graduate, find work, find a person, buy a home, become certain. The neatness of this sequence is comforting, which is perhaps why it survives contact with so little evidence.

Most lives are assembled more like a desk drawer. They contain useful things, sentimental things, and several objects whose purpose is unclear but which you are not ready to throw away. A wrong turn is not proof that you have failed to begin. It is information.

The years ahead do not need you to arrive fully formed. They need your attention. Pay attention to what makes a day feel spacious. Notice the work that leaves you tired in a satisfying way. Keep track of the people around whom your voice sounds like your own.

This is not a productivity system. It is permission to be present for the life you are actually living.

## A life can be in progress

There are choices you will make because they are right, and choices you will make because they are available. Both kinds can teach you something. Neither needs to become a permanent definition.

The point is not to stop wanting more. The point is to stop treating the current version of your life as an embarrassing draft that nobody should see.

You are allowed to be underway.
