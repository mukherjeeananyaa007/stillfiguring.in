For a long time, I believed there was a moment coming when I would finally *know*. Know what I wanted, who I was, and what I was doing with my life. I pictured it like a door: once I walked through, everything on the other side would feel settled.

So I waited. I told myself I’d start properly once I had clarity. I’d commit once I was sure. I’d speak up once I felt ready. Meanwhile, I treated the present as a waiting room, somewhere to sit politely until my real life was called.

The strange thing is that the door never showed up. What showed up instead were days. Ordinary ones, where I made small decisions with incomplete information and lived with the results. Some of those decisions were wrong. A few of them turned out to be the best things I ever did, and I only knew that much later.

I’ve started to suspect that clarity doesn’t come before action. It comes *from* it. You don’t figure out what you like by thinking about it for a year. You figure it out by trying something, noticing how you feel at the end of the day, and adjusting. The self I was waiting for wasn’t hiding somewhere ahead of me. She was being built, slowly, out of exactly the choices I kept postponing.

That doesn’t make uncertainty comfortable. It still shows up at odd hours, usually when everyone else seems to have a plan. Scrolling makes it worse. Everyone’s life looks like a finished sentence, and mine feels like a draft full of strikethroughs.

But drafts are where the work happens. Nobody sees the strikethroughs in a finished piece, and nobody mistakes the final version for the thinking that produced it. I’m trying to extend myself the same courtesy: to be someone in progress without apologising for it.

So here is what I’m practising these days:

- Making the smaller decision instead of waiting for the perfect one.
- Letting “I don’t know yet” be a complete sentence.
- Treating today as the real thing, not rehearsal.

I don’t think I’ll ever feel fully finished, and I’m slowly making peace with that. Maybe being *still figuring* isn’t a phase to get through. Maybe it’s just what being alive and paying attention feels like.

If you’re also standing in the waiting room, I hope this is a small nudge to walk out. Nobody is going to call your name. You can just start.
