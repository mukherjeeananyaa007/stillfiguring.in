There is a strange kind of loneliness in not knowing what you want from your life. Especially when you're at an age when it feels like everyone else does.

Your friends are building careers, getting promotions, moving cities, falling in love, making plans. Everyone seems to be going somewhere. And then there's you, wondering if you're even walking in the right direction.

It's not that things are necessarily bad. You might have a good job, people who love you, a life that looks perfectly fine from the outside. You might even be proud of how far you've come. But somewhere between everything you've achieved and everything you're supposed to want next, you've lost the ability to tell what you actually want.

I think that's the confusing part about your 20s. For the longest time, life comes with a fairly predictable set of milestones. Do well in school. Get into a good college. Find a job. Become someone you're proud of. There is always a next thing to work towards, and you're rarely asked whether you actually want it.

Then one day, the milestones stop feeling like enough. You get some of the things you once wished for, and instead of feeling fulfilled, you find yourself looking for the next thing. Not necessarily because you want it, but because you're so used to moving towards something that standing still feels uncomfortable.

Maybe we've spent so much time becoming people others would be proud of that we've forgotten to ask what would make us happy.

And perhaps being lost isn't always about having no direction. Sometimes it's about having too many directions, too many expectations, and too many versions of yourself that you think you could become.

I'm trying to remind myself that I don't have to turn every interest into a career, every opportunity into an achievement, or every year into a measurable improvement over the last. Not everything has to lead somewhere. Some things can just be things I enjoy.

I don't think there's a sudden moment when you figure it all out. Maybe you just start paying closer attention. To what makes you feel alive, what drains you, what you keep coming back to when nobody is watching.

And maybe that's enough for now.

I am beginning to think that the point of your 20s isn't to have your life figured out. It's to get to know the person who has to live it.

And that's probably going to take a little longer than we expected.
