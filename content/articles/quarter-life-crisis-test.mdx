It's 2 a.m. and you're scrolling through a former classmate's promotion announcement, quietly wondering how everyone else seems to have gotten a map you never received. You have a job, a salary, maybe even a decent apartment. On paper, things are fine. So why does it feel like you're standing in a hallway full of doors, unable to tell which one is yours?

If that sounds familiar, you've probably typed "quarter life crisis test" into a search bar at some point. This post is a gentle, honest version of that test, plus what to do with the result.

## What a quarter life crisis actually is

A quarter life crisis is a stretch of doubt, restlessness and self-questioning, usually somewhere between your early twenties and early thirties, about your career, relationships, identity and direction. It isn't a clinical diagnosis, and it isn't a sign that you've failed.

It tends to show up when the structure of student life ends and nobody replaces it. For years, the next step was obvious: the next exam, the next degree, the next campus placement. Then the steps stop, and you're expected to design your own.

What it is **not**: depression, burnout or anxiety, though it can overlap with all three. If you feel persistently hopeless, can't function day to day, or have thoughts of harming yourself, that deserves a conversation with a doctor or therapist, not a blog quiz.

## The quarter life crisis test

Think about the last three months. Score each statement from 0 to 3: **0** = never, **1** = sometimes, **2** = often, **3** = almost always. Go with your gut, not the answer you think you should give.

1. I wonder whether I chose the wrong career or degree.
2. I compare my life to other people's and come out behind.
3. I feel like I should be further along by now.
4. I feel stuck, even though nothing is obviously wrong.
5. I'm unsure what I actually want, not just what I'm supposed to want.
6. Sunday evenings fill me with dread.
7. I fantasize about starting over somewhere else, in a new job, city or life.
8. I feel like I'm performing a version of adulthood rather than living it.
9. I'm unhappy with my current friendships or relationships, or feel lonely in them.
10. I struggle to make decisions because every option feels like the wrong one.
11. I feel anxious about money, stability or the future more than I admit to others.
12. I have moments of real clarity and excitement, then they fade quickly.

Add up your score. The maximum is 36.

## What your score means

This is a reflection tool, not a diagnosis. Treat the bands as a mirror, not a verdict.

- **0–9: Steady ground.** Normal doubts, nothing urgent. You're mostly aligned with your life right now.
- **10–18: Healthy questioning.** You're asking real questions about direction. Uncomfortable, but useful.
- **19–27: Full quarter life crisis.** Your current path and your inner compass are clearly pulling apart. Worth acting on.
- **28–36: Heavy weather.** The doubt is taking up most of your headspace. Consider talking to a therapist or counsellor, alongside the steps below.

A high score doesn't mean something is wrong with you. It often means you're paying attention to a gap between the life you're living and the one you want.

## What to do next

You don't need to blow up your life. Small, honest experiments beat dramatic exits.

- **Find your highest-scoring statements.** Two or three of them usually point to the real issue, whether that's work, relationships or money. Start there, not with all twelve.
- **Separate "what I want" from "what I'm supposed to want."** Write down one thing you'd do if nobody was watching or comparing. Notice how hard that is to answer, and what it tells you.
- **Run a small experiment.** Take a weekend course, shadow someone in a field you're curious about, or start a side project. Treat it as data collection, not a commitment.
- **Cut the comparison feed.** Mute or limit the accounts that trigger the "I'm behind" feeling for two weeks and see what changes.
- **Talk to someone one step ahead.** People five to ten years older are usually more honest about their own wobbles than social media suggests.
- **Get structure back.** Regular sleep, movement and one routine you look forward to make big questions easier to think about.
- **Know when to get help.** If your score is in the top band, or the low mood doesn't lift, speak to a counsellor. It's a sensible move, not an overreaction.

## One last thing

A quarter life crisis feels like falling behind, but it's closer to a checkpoint. It's the first time you get to ask whether the life you built, or inherited, still fits the person you're becoming.

You don't have to have the answer this year. You only have to keep asking honestly, and keep moving in small ways while you do. Not knowing yet is part of the process, not proof that you're failing at it.
